Self, Relationship and Couple Therapy Research Corpus

Version 2.0

A theory-plural research corpus for self-reflection, relationship formulation, couple-process analysis, therapy-model comparison, rupture and repair, values-based decision clarification, intergenerational family-systems analysis, and disciplined use of dreams and depth-psychological interpretation.

Canonical corpus files

  1. 00 - Corpus Constitution, Evidence Hierarchy and Source Router Governing rules, evidence model, terminology, safety rules, framework boundaries, and source routing.
  2. 01 - Self-Agency, Attachment, Mentalization and Emotion Self-agency, attachment, working models, mentalization, emotion regulation, autonomy, identity, and relational self-states.
  3. 02 - Relational Patterns, Couple Systems, Intimacy and Power Dyadic patterns, relationship science, trust, responsiveness, intimacy, sexuality, ordinary relationship practices, context, and power.
  4. 03 - Couple Therapy Models, Mechanisms and Evidence Comparative map of major couple-therapy models, mechanisms, procedures, treatment evidence, overlap, and limits.
  5. 04 - Rupture, Betrayal, Repair, Boundaries and Endings Attachment injury, betrayal, trust, accountability, forgiveness, repair, safety, boundaries, incompatibility, separation, and endings.
  6. 05 - Self-Analysis, Reflective Methods, Dreams and Formulation Self-observation, episode reconstruction, journaling, longitudinal tracking, behavioural tests, dream work, and reflective methods.
  7. 06 - Integrated Applied Formulation and Couple Cycle Lab Authoritative applied layer for episode analysis, couple-cycle maps, competing hypotheses, practical tests, and decision clarification.
  8. 07 - Values, Agency, Choice, Meaning and Relational Ethics Psychological flexibility, values, acceptance, existential choice, meaning, relational ethics, trade-offs, and committed action.
  9. 08 - Family Systems, Intergenerational Patterns and Differentiation Family-of-origin mapping, differentiation, triangles, emotional cutoff, multigenerational patterns, family roles, and contextual guardrails.
